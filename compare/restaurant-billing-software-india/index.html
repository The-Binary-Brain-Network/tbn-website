Restaurant billing software in India: how to actually choose.

We build one of these products, so read this with that in mind. We've still written the guide we wish existed when we were the ones buying — including the parts where our own software is the wrong answer.

Short answer

There is no single best restaurant billing software in India. The honest answer turns on three questions: is your internet reliable, how many outlets do you run, and would you rather pay every month forever or once a year.

If connectivity is dependable and you need deep Swiggy and Zomato integration today, an established cloud POS is the reasonable choice. If your restaurant has to keep billing when the connection drops — which in most of India it will — an offline-first annual licence is the safer default.

Judge any candidate on four things: does billing work with the internet physically unplugged, what does three years cost including every add-on you'll actually use, can you export your own data without asking permission, and is bill numbering sequential and audit-safe.

Most comparison articles on this subject are written by whoever is selling. This one is too — we make Pass. What we can offer instead of false neutrality is specificity: real numbers, the questions that actually separate these products, and a clear statement of where Pass loses.

The Real Decision

You're choosing a licence model, not a feature list.

Feature lists across this category converge fast — everyone does KOTs, everyone does GST, everyone does reports. What genuinely differs is how you pay and where your data lives, and those two choices outlast every feature comparison you'll make.

The mistake worth avoiding is choosing cloud by default because it's what everyone pitches, without having asked what happens to your evening when it's unreachable.

Before You Sign

Six questions worth more than a feature demo.

Ask these of every vendor including us. The answers separate products far more reliably than a features grid does.

01

"Can I unplug the internet and take a bill?"

Ask them to demonstrate it, not describe it. "Works offline" often means orders queue and sync later — which is no help at all if the counter itself is frozen. The test is whether you can complete a sale and print, with the router switched off.

02

"What does three years cost, everything included?"

Get it in writing with every add-on you'll genuinely use. Kitchen display screens, loyalty, customer feedback, extra terminals and aggregator integrations are frequently priced apart from the headline number, and the headline is what gets quoted.

03

"How do I get all my data out?"

The right answer is a button you press yourself. If it's "raise a request with support", you're renting access to your own sales history. Ask specifically about menu, bills, customers and stock — not just a summary report.

04

"Is bill numbering sequential and audit-safe?"

Numbering must not reset mid-year, and a correction must create a new linked document with a reason logged rather than silently editing the original. This is the difference between a clean GST audit and an uncomfortable afternoon.

05

"Will it drive the printer I already own?"

Most Indian restaurants have an 80mm thermal printer already. Confirm it prints silently — with no dialog box appearing mid-service — and that KOT and bill printers can be set separately.

06

"Who answers at 10pm on Saturday?"

Not what the SLA document claims. Ask for the actual channel and a realistic response time during service. A ticket queue read on Monday morning is not support for a business that trades at weekends.

Answers

Questions buyers ask us.

What is the best restaurant billing software in India?
There isn't one best — it depends on your connectivity, your outlet count, and whether you prefer monthly or annual payment. With reliable internet and a need for deep aggregator integration, an established cloud POS is reasonable. If billing must survive an outage, or you object to renting software indefinitely, an offline-first annual licence fits better. Judge on the four things above: offline billing, three-year total cost, data portability, and audit-safe numbering.
How much should restaurant POS software cost in India?
Around ₹6,000–₹9,000 per year for a self-hosted annual licence, or ₹800–₹2,500 per month for cloud SaaS. Over three years, a mid-tier subscription at ₹1,500/month reaches about ₹54,000 against roughly ₹22,500 for an annual licence at ₹7,500. Always ask for a three-year total including add-ons — that's where the gap widens.
Is offline POS actually better than cloud?
Better for reliability, worse for multi-location reporting. Offline-first keeps billing when the connection dies. Cloud gives live consolidated reporting and easy remote access. For a single restaurant or small group in India, where connectivity is usually the weak link, offline-first is the safer default. For a large chain with solid connectivity and central operations, cloud has genuine advantages.
Do I need composite GST scheme support?
Only if you're registered under the composition scheme — turnover up to ₹1.5 crore. If so you must issue a Bill of Supply rather than a tax invoice, must not show a GST breakup, and must print "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies". Confirm your chosen software produces that exact document; many handle only regular-scheme invoices. Ours is adding it — today Pass handles regular-scheme flat and per-item GST. More detail in our glossary entry.
Can I run Pass on more than one outlet?
Yes, and unlimited outlets are included in the single ₹7,500 annual licence at current pricing. Each keeps its own GSTIN, FSSAI, address and — importantly — its own bill numbering sequence, because two outlets sharing a sequence would issue duplicate invoice numbers, which is a GST problem. The menu stays central so a price change lands everywhere at once.
What about hotels rather than restaurants?
Different problem, different software. For a hotel needing room-service billing alongside an existing property management system, see Folio. For a full front-desk system — bookings, folios, housekeeping, C-Form — see Slate. There's a separate guide to choosing hotel management software in India.
