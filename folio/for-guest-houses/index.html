Why Guest Houses Are Different

Four things enterprise POS gets wrong at this scale.

A guest house does 3–15 F&B orders a day, is often single-staffed, runs mostly on cash, and cannot afford software that needs training, IT support or an internet contract. Enterprise POS is a mismatch on every dimension.

01 · SIMPLE

The tool has to disappear

Owner-operators don't do software training courses. The software has to be usable on day one, by whoever happens to be at the desk when the order comes in — including a family member covering a shift.

2-minute onboarding wizard sets up the property. Menu entry is a spreadsheet-like grid. Placing an order is 3 clicks. Help is one keystroke away.
02 · CASH

Cash is still the default

Not every guest house takes cards. UPI is common, but cash still dominates. The software has to make cash change calculation, cash-in-hand tracking and end-of-day cash reconciliation trivial.

Colour-coded cash change calculator. Day-close report auto-prints with cash summary. Manager PIN protects sensitive actions. Handover log tracks who closed which day.
03 · OFFLINE

The internet is a bonus, not a base

A hill-station guest house may lose internet for a whole afternoon. The billing system cannot depend on it. Cloud POS that queues everything for later sync is a lie — if the counter is jammed, the sync doesn't help.

100% offline. Orders, bills, KOTs, printing, reports — all local. Internet is used only for optional bill-share on WhatsApp and silent product updates. Never blocks operations.
04 · PRICE

The software cost has to be one line

A 12-room guest house cannot justify a monthly SaaS bill on top of internet, electricity, staff wages and licence renewals. Every extra recurring line is a fight with the accountant.

Flat ₹6,500 per property per year. One line on the books. No per-seat fees, no add-on tiers, no transaction charges. Renewal at the same price, quoted before you sign.
