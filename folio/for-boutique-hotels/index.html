Why Boutique Is Different

Four things generic hotel POS gets wrong at this scale.

A boutique property (10–40 rooms) sits in an awkward gap — too small to run enterprise PMS + POS + KDS stacks, too polished to run a paper notebook. Folio is built for exactly this scale.

01 · GUEST

Multi-day tabs, not multi-visit orders

A guest stays three nights and orders eight times. Every order needs to accumulate against the room tab, not become an isolated bill they see at each order. Restaurant POS closes the tab too fast; hotel PMS doesn't do F&B at all.

Tab per room opens at check-in and stays open through the stay. Every order adds to the running total. Guest sees a single itemised bill at checkout with all charges shown chronologically.
02 · BILL

The print goes to a paying guest

A restaurant bill can be a rough thermal receipt. A boutique-hotel bill is customer-facing hospitality — it gets photographed, it goes into an expense claim, it is the last written record of your service. It has to look like you meant it.

Thermal 80mm bills with your hotel header, GSTIN, FSSAI, room, dates, itemised list, tax breakup and total. Reprints are identical to the original. Guest and hotel copies print together.
03 · MENU

A tight menu is the point

Boutique hotels don't run 200-item menus. They run 20-40 curated items with careful pricing, dietary flags and clear descriptions. Cluttered POS with categories, sub-categories and modifier stacks is a mismatch to the operation.

Flat, editable menu. Categories are optional. Items load fast, search is one-key. Availability toggle for when the chef is out of an item. The tool disappears at the operator level.
04 · OFFLINE

Small hotels have big internet problems

Hill-station, coastal and heritage properties have flaky internet that drops for hours. A cloud POS that stops taking orders during a Saturday-night monsoon is worse than a paper notepad.

Fully offline. Orders, KOTs, bills, printing, reports and reprints all work with zero internet. Hourly encrypted backups. Auto-recovery if a device fails. Data lives on your machine.
