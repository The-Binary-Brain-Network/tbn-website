Why Cafes Are Different

The four things generic POS gets wrong for coffee.

Cafe operations have their own rhythm — high volume, low ticket size, wide menu variance, and a customer base that mostly comes back. Software built for full-service restaurants misses all four of these.

01 · MENU

Size variants aren't optional

A cappuccino is a Regular or a Large, an iced tea is a Small or a Medium. Every drink has 2–3 SKUs. Restaurant POS treats these as separate menu items, blowing up your menu into 300 entries and destroying reporting.

One menu item ("Cappuccino") with Regular / Large variants at their own prices. Reports still roll up under Cappuccino, so you know which drinks sell — not which sizes.
02 · INVENTORY

Milk runs out on Sunday morning, not at end of day

Cafe stock is dominated by milk, coffee beans and syrups — high-frequency, high-cost items. You need to know at 10am that you'll be out of oat milk by 2pm, not at the day-close report.

Menu items link to ingredients with per-serve quantities. Every drink deducts stock automatically. Low-stock alerts fire in real time at your configurable threshold.
03 · LOYALTY

70% of your revenue is repeat customers

A cafe's business is regulars. A guest who comes twice a week for six months is worth ₹15k. You cannot afford to treat them like walk-ins. Loyalty is not a "nice to have" here — it is the business.

Phone-based customer database with visit count, total spend, birthday, and points. Points earnable and redeemable at the counter as a payment mode. Nothing to install for the customer.
04 · PEAK HOURS

The 8am rush breaks slow software

Between 8:15 and 9:45, one barista is taking 40 orders in 90 minutes. The POS cannot be slow, cannot need extra clicks, cannot wait for the internet to respond. Cloud SaaS with a poor connection is a lost customer.

Fully offline core flow. Type-ahead item search. One-click size + modifier selection. Keyboard shortcuts for common orders. Silent thermal printing — no dialog interrupts anything.
