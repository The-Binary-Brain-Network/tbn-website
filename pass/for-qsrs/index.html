The QSR Reality

Four things generic POS gets wrong for quick-service.

A QSR is a stopwatch business. Every extra second at the counter is a customer walking to the next stall. Restaurant POS built for table service optimises the wrong variables.

01 · SPEED

The counter is timed to the second

A busy QSR does an order every 25–40 seconds. Cloud POS that waits for the internet, table-POS that asks which table, or software that needs three clicks to punch a bill is losing you real money every shift.

Quick Order Entry mode with type-ahead search. Direct-to-KOT for takeaway. Zero table selection required for counter orders. Fully offline — never waits for a network.
02 · MIX

Dine-in, takeaway and delivery on one screen

A typical urban QSR runs 40% dine-in, 40% takeaway, 20% delivery. Switching modes cannot be an interruption. Restaurant POS forces you into a "table select" flow that assumes dine-in is default.

Single screen with three order modes as top-level buttons. Delivery orders capture phone + address, prints a delivery slip alongside the KOT.
03 · KOT

Kitchen queue is a workflow, not a display

The kitchen doesn't want a screen showing 40 open orders. It wants one KOT per order, printed instantly, with the item + quantity + any modifier. Bells and whistles slow the line.

Silent thermal-printed KOTs, auto-numbered, on 80mm paper. Per-station printer routing — Tandoor items to Tandoor printer, Chinese to Chinese. No display distraction.
04 · UPI

UPI is 60% of your payments

In a QSR, more than half of counter payments are UPI QR. The QR needs to appear instantly with the bill total pre-filled, and the payment needs to auto-confirm — waiting for the customer to shout "done" is a delay.

Dynamic UPI QR generated per bill with the amount encoded. Auto-polls for payment confirmation every 3 seconds. Cash change calculator and card support built in.
