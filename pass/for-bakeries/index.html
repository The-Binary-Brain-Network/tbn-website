Why Bakeries Are Different

Four things a bakery needs from its POS.

A bakery sells in units nobody else does — grams, dozens, half-kilo cakes — and loses money in a place restaurants rarely look: what didn't sell yesterday.

01 · UNITS

Everything is sold in three different ways

A rusks packet is a piece. Butter cookies are per 100g. A cake is 1/2 kg or 1 kg or 2 kg. Bread is a loaf, half loaf or slices. One menu item, three or four SKUs, all with independent pricing.

Menu items with size / weight variants. Per-variant pricing, per-variant stock deduction. Reports still roll up under the parent item so you know your top sellers by product, not by SKU.
02 · RECIPES

A cake is twelve ingredients

You buy flour by the sack and butter by the kilo, but sell by the piece. Without recipes, stock is a guess until the day you run out mid-bake.

Link each item to its ingredients with a quantity per piece or per kilo. Every sale deducts stock automatically, low-stock alerts fire at your threshold, and purchase orders from suppliers receive straight into stock.
03 · COUNTER

The evening rush is a queue

Bread at 6pm, cakes for the weekend, a packet of rusks — a counter that hesitates loses the sale to the bakery down the road.

Keyboard type-ahead item search, sold-out toggles that grey items out instantly, UPI QR on the counter, and silent thermal printing. Fully offline, so a dead connection never stops billing.
04 · WASTAGE

Yesterday's croissants are today's loss

Bakery inventory has a shelf life measured in days. What didn't sell yesterday is wastage today, and unless you track it, you'll never know which items are structurally over-produced. This is the number that decides whether a bakery is profitable.

Record wastage as its own stock movement with notes, so it sits in the stock log next to purchases and sales instead of disappearing. Expenses and P&L export to Excel for the accountant.

Multi-rate items on the same bill

Bakery items span 0%, 5%, 12% and 18% GST slabs depending on packaging and category. Pass sets tax rate per item, so a single bill can carry multiple correctly-taxed lines.
