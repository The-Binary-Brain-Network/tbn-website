Why "night" audit

Hotels run on a business date that does not quite match the calendar. A guest checking in at 1am on the 15th is usually part of the 14th's night. The night audit is the moment the hotel declares one business day finished and the next begun — traditionally done in the quiet hours after midnight, hence the name.

What the audit does, step by step

1. Posts room charges

For every occupied room, the night's room rate (and its GST) is posted to the guest's folio. This is the single most important step. If it does not happen, a guest who stays four nights may be billed for three, or someone has to add the charges by hand at check-out.

2. Handles no-shows

Bookings due to arrive today that never turned up are marked no-show, releasing the room in your inventory and, where your policy allows, triggering a no-show charge.

3. Reconciles the day

Charges posted and payments taken are checked against each other, and against the cash and UPI the desk actually holds. This is where the cash reconciliation happens for a hotel.

4. Flags exceptions

Departures with an unpaid balance, rooms marked occupied with no booking, foreign guests without a submitted Form-III, rooms out of order — anything a manager should look at before the day is locked.

5. Snapshots the day

Occupancy, average room rate, revenue by source and similar figures are frozen, so yesterday's report says the same thing next week.

6. Rolls the business date

The hotel moves to the next business day.

Automatic or manager-confirmed?

Software can run the audit automatically at a set time. That is convenient, and it guarantees room charges are always posted. The risk is that exceptions get locked in without anyone seeing them. The alternative is a manager-confirmed close: the software prepares everything and shows the exceptions; a manager reviews and confirms.

A sensible approach for most small hotels: let the software prepare the audit automatically, and have the duty manager confirm it each morning with the exceptions list open.

What if the audit is missed?

A property that is closed for a few days, or a PC that was switched off, can miss several audits. Good software catches up: it runs each missed day in order, posting the right charges to the right dates, rather than lumping everything onto today.

The reports you should read every morning

  • Night audit summary — what was posted, what was flagged.
  • Revenue summary for yesterday — rooms, F&B, other.
  • Occupancy and the forecast for the coming days.
  • Outstanding dues — who owes what.
  • No-shows and cancellations.

A morning review in ten minutes

Whoever opens the hotel each morning — owner, manager or senior receptionist — should spend ten minutes on yesterday:

  1. Open the night audit summary. Were all occupied rooms charged?
  2. Read the exceptions list: unpaid departures, no-shows, rooms occupied without bookings, foreign guests without forms.
  3. Check the revenue summary against the cash and UPI handed over.
  4. Look at today's arrivals and the occupancy forecast.
  5. Note anything that needs a follow-up call — a company that owes money, a no-show to charge.

Signs your hotel is skipping the audit

  • Guests' bills at check-out are missing nights.
  • Yesterday's occupancy figure changes when you look again next week.
  • No-show bookings still sit on the room board days later.
  • Month-end revenue does not match the sum of the daily reports.

Any of these means the business day is not being closed consistently.
