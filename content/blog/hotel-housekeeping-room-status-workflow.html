Start with the status codes

Keep the list short enough that every housekeeper uses it the same way:

StatusMeansCan the desk sell it?
OccupiedA guest is in residenceNo
Due outGuest expected to leave todayFor later today, once cleaned
Vacant dirtyGuest has left; not yet cleanedNo
Vacant cleanCleaned, awaiting inspectionIf you skip inspection
InspectedChecked and readyYes
Out of orderRepair neededNo — and should not be offered online

Small hotels often merge "vacant clean" and "inspected". That is fine if the person cleaning is trusted to check the minibar, the bathroom and the AC remote.

The daily rhythm

  1. Morning assignment. The supervisor (or the desk, in a small property) assigns rooms: departures first, then stay-overs. Departures matter most because they become sellable rooms.
  2. Clean and update. Each housekeeper marks a room clean from their phone the moment they finish — not at the end of the shift.
  3. Inspect. The supervisor checks and marks inspected.
  4. Report problems. A leaking tap, a broken lamp — logged on the spot with the room number, so maintenance has a list and the room can be marked out of order if needed.
  5. Evening check. Any room still dirty is flagged before the evening arrivals.

Why phones beat paper here

The paper status sheet is correct at 9am and wrong by 11. The desk ends up calling the floor or walking up to check. When status is updated from a phone in the room, the front-desk room board is current to the minute — which is what lets you sell the 2pm early check-in, or an hourly stay, with confidence.

Out of order is a sales issue too

A room under repair must come off every channel. If you sell on OTAs, an out-of-order room still showing as available invites a booking you cannot honour. Make sure "out of order" in your housekeeping system also removes the room from what you offer online.

Laundry: the quiet leak

Linen and towels disappear slowly — damaged, stained, lost at the laundry. Track every issue to the laundry and every return, by item and quantity. A monthly reconcile shows what is at the laundry, what you are spending, and what has gone missing. Without it, you simply buy new towels more often than you should.

Measuring housekeeping

  • Rooms cleaned per housekeeper per shift.
  • Average time from check-out to inspected.
  • Rooms out of order, and for how long.
  • Maintenance issues opened and closed.

The point is not to squeeze staff; it is to see whether you have enough of them on busy days.

Inspection: a five-minute checklist

An inspection catches what a hurried clean misses. Keep it short enough to actually happen:

  • Bed made, linen fresh and unstained.
  • Bathroom clean and dry; towels, soap and toiletries stocked.
  • Floor clean, bins empty.
  • AC, TV, lights and remote working.
  • Water bottles and amenities replenished.
  • Nothing left by the previous guest.

Anything broken goes straight into the maintenance log, with the room marked out of order if it cannot be sold.

Stay-over rooms

Occupied rooms need servicing too, usually with less work and at a time that suits the guest. Ask at check-in whether guests want daily service, and record "do not disturb" requests so housekeeping does not knock at 9am. Some hotels offer a small credit or donation for guests who skip daily service; if you do, track it so it reaches the folio.

Staffing on busy days

As a rough planning number, one housekeeper cleans perhaps 12 to 16 standard rooms in a shift, fewer for departures than stay-overs. On a wedding weekend with 20 departures and 20 arrivals, plan the day's assignments the evening before, and stagger late check-outs so rooms are not all ready at 4pm. A productivity report tells you over time what your own team actually achieves.
