The four essentials

1. A booking calendar that cannot double-book

The most expensive mistake a small property makes is selling the same room twice — usually once over the phone and once through an OTA. A single calendar that every booking goes into, and that shows clearly which rooms are taken, solves 90% of it. If you sell on two or more OTAs, look at channel management (see do small hotels need a channel manager?).

2. A proper bill

If you are GST-registered, room bills need your GSTIN, a sequential invoice number, and the right rate: 5% without input tax credit for rooms charged up to ₹7,500 a night, 18% above. If you are not registered, issue a simple numbered bill without tax. See GST on hotel rooms.

3. Foreign guest reporting

Every foreign guest — including OCI cardholders under the 2025 rules — must be reported on Form-III within 24 hours. A homestay is not exempt. Capture passport and visa details at check-in, and keep a record of each submission. Our Form-III guide has the checklist.

4. Food and extras on the room

Breakfast, dinners, laundry, a bike rental. Small amounts, easy to forget, and the source of most check-out disputes. Keep a running tab per room, not a note in a diary.

Nice to have

  • Guest history, so a returning guest's details fill in.
  • An owner's daily summary.
  • Housekeeping status on a phone, once you have staff.
  • Expense tracking.

What you can skip (for now)

  • Revenue management and dynamic pricing tools.
  • Complex rate plans — a high season, a low season and a weekend rate cover most homestays.
  • Multi-property dashboards.
  • Point-of-sale hardware beyond a small thermal printer.

Offline matters more in small places

Guest houses and homestays are often where connectivity is weakest — hills, beaches, villages. Software that cannot issue a bill without the internet is a poor fit. Look for tools that keep working offline and back themselves up automatically.

A daily routine for a small property

  1. Morning: check today's departures and arrivals; make sure every departing room's food and extras are on its bill.
  2. Check-out: settle the bill, issue the invoice, mark the room for cleaning.
  3. Afternoon: update OTA availability for any rooms booked by phone overnight.
  4. Check-in: collect ID; for foreign guests, capture passport and visa details and file Form-III the same day.
  5. Evening: record the day's cash and UPI; check tomorrow's bookings.

Fifteen minutes a day, done consistently, is worth more than any software feature.

Mistakes small properties make

  • Taking phone bookings on paper and forgetting to block the room online.
  • Breakfast and dinners noted in a diary, missed at check-out.
  • No numbered bills, so there is no record if a guest disputes a charge later.
  • Foreign guest forms "done later".
  • Everything on the owner's phone — and nothing when the phone is lost.

Budgeting for software

For a small property, software should cost a small fraction of a single night's revenue per month. As a guide, five-room properties often do well with free or very low-cost tools; ten to twenty-room properties benefit from a proper system, and should compare five-year costs between per-room subscriptions and flat licences.
