Blank or very faint prints

Cause 1: the roll is upside down. Thermal paper darkens only on its coated side. Scratch the paper with a fingernail; the side that marks grey is the printing side. Load the roll so that side faces the print head — on most printers, paper feeds from underneath the roll.

Cause 2: old or wrong paper. Thermal paper fades with heat and age, and plain paper rolls of the same size will not print at all. Buy from a supplier you trust and store rolls away from the kitchen heat.

Cause 3: print density. Some drivers have a density or darkness setting. Raise it one step if prints are pale but readable.

Cause 4: a dirty head. Kitchens coat everything in grease. Switch off, let the head cool, and wipe it with isopropyl alcohol on a lint-free cloth.

Paper doesn't cut

  • Open the printer's driver preferences and enable cut after each document (wording varies).
  • Some inexpensive printers' drivers ignore the cut command from applications. Billing software that sends the cut command directly to the printer — a "force cut" or raw-print option — solves this.
  • If the cutter jams, switch off, open the cover and remove any paper scraps around the blade.

Text cut off at the edges, or a narrow strip of text

This is almost always a width mismatch.

  • An 80mm roll has about 72mm printable; a 58mm roll about 48mm. If the software thinks it is printing 80mm on a 58mm printer, the right side is lost; the other way round, you get a thin column in the middle.
  • Check the paper size in the printer driver, then the paper width in your billing software. They must agree.
  • If a few characters are clipped on one side only, adjust the left or right margin in the software. An alignment test page with a printed ruler makes this a one-minute job.

Not sure which size you have? Measure the roll. 58mm versus 80mm explains the trade-offs.

Printer shows offline

USB printers

  • Try a different USB port, directly on the PC rather than through a hub.
  • Check the printer is switched on and the cover is closed — many printers report "offline" when the cover is open or paper is out.
  • In the operating system's printer list, make sure the right printer is not paused and "use printer offline" is not ticked.

Network printers

  • Print the printer's self-test page (usually by holding the feed button while switching on). It shows the current IP address.
  • If the address has changed since setup, your router reassigned it — common after power cuts. In the router settings, reserve a fixed IP for the printer's MAC address.
  • Prefer Ethernet over Wi-Fi in kitchens; steel and tiles absorb signals.

Prints going to the wrong printer

When a restaurant has a bill printer and several KOT printers, it is easy to map a station to the wrong one. Name printers clearly in the operating system — BILL-COUNTER, KOT-TANDOOR — then recheck the mapping in the POS. Send a test order that touches every station.

Duplicate prints

Usually two causes: the bill is set to print automatically after payment and someone presses print; or both the customer copy and an internal copy are enabled when only one is needed. Check the auto-print and copies settings.

Garbled characters or symbols

Random characters usually mean the wrong driver — a generic text driver sending data the printer does not understand. Install the manufacturer's driver, or choose the ESC/POS driver your billing software recommends. A missing ₹ symbol is a character-set issue; many printers cannot print it, and "Rs." is an acceptable fallback on a bill.

A preventive checklist

  • Keep two spare rolls per printer, stored away from heat.
  • Reserve fixed IPs for every network printer.
  • Put printers on a UPS along with the billing PC.
  • Clean print heads monthly in the kitchen.
  • Keep the alignment test page from setup, so you can spot drift.
