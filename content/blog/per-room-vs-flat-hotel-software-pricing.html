The three pricing models

  • Per room per month — the most common cloud PMS model. You pay a fee for each room, every month.
  • Per device or per user — a fee for each computer, tablet or login. Sometimes layered on top of per-room.
  • Flat annual licence — one price per property per year, regardless of rooms or devices.

At ₹50 per room, the break-even point moves to 20 rooms; at ₹200, to 5. The shape is the same: per-room pricing grows in a straight line with your hotel, flat pricing does not.

The expansion test

Suppose a 20-room hotel adds a 12-room wing in year three. On per-room pricing, the software bill rises 60% the month the wing opens — at exactly the moment the owner is paying for construction. On flat pricing, nothing changes. Ask any vendor: what will I pay if I add ten rooms?

The devices test

Modern hotel operations put the system on phones: housekeeping updating room status, the manager checking arrivals, a tablet at the restaurant. Per-device pricing makes every one of those a cost decision. Many hotels end up rationing access, which defeats the point.

Costs that are not in the headline

  • Setup, data migration and training fees.
  • Channel manager — often a separate subscription.
  • Booking engine for your own website.
  • Payment gateway integration.
  • Premium support or "priority" plans.
  • Price increase at renewal — is it capped in writing?
  • Exporting your data when you leave.

When per-room pricing makes sense

To be fair to it: per-room pricing is genuinely cheaper for a very small property — a five-room homestay at ₹50 per room pays ₹3,000 a year. It also usually includes cloud hosting and access from anywhere. If you are small, staying small and value remote access, it can be the right choice. Run your own numbers in the cost calculator.

What flat pricing should include

A flat licence is only good value if "flat" means flat. Check that it covers:

  • Every module — not a "flat" core with paid add-ons.
  • Every device on your property.
  • Installation and training.
  • Updates for the year.

How to read a PMS quote

Quotes are rarely laid out for comparison. Rewrite each one into the same five lines:

  1. Year-one total, including setup.
  2. Annual cost from year two, at your current room count.
  3. Annual cost if you add ten rooms.
  4. Add-ons you need — channel manager, booking engine, POS, extra devices — each with its price.
  5. Renewal terms — is the price fixed, capped or open?

Multiply out five years. The cheapest year-one quote is often not the cheapest five-year one.

Negotiating

  • Ask for multi-year pricing to be fixed in writing.
  • Ask for setup and training to be included.
  • Ask whether off-season or partial-year properties pay less.
  • Ask what happens to price if you add rooms mid-contract.
  • Ask for data export to be free and self-service.
