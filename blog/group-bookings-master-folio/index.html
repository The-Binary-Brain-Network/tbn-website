Group bookings and the master folio: billing a wedding party without chaos.

A wedding party books fifteen rooms. The bride's family pays for the rooms, each guest pays for their own minibar, and the uncle in 204 wants his dinner on the family's bill. A master folio is how a hotel keeps that straight.

Short answer

A master folio is a single bill that collects the charges a group's organiser has agreed to pay — typically room rent and shared meals — while each room keeps its own folio for personal extras. When the group books, block the rooms under one group, decide which charges route to the master folio, and settle the master folio with the organiser at check-out.

Before the group arrives

Agree who pays for what — in writing

Most group billing disputes start because "the family is paying" meant different things to different people. Agree with the organiser, before arrival:

  • Room rent: master or individual?
  • Breakfast and group meals: master?
  • Room service, minibar, laundry: individual?
  • Extra beds, early check-in, late check-out: who approves, who pays?
  • Advance paid, and the payment schedule for the balance.
  • The organiser's GSTIN, if they want a business invoice.

Block the rooms

Create one group booking with all the rooms, so availability, the room board and reports treat them as one party. Set a release date: rooms not confirmed by then go back on general sale.

During the stay: routing charges

Every charge posted during the stay goes to one of two places:

  • The master folio — anything the organiser agreed to pay.
  • The room folio — the guest's own extras.

The desk needs to see, when posting a charge, which folio it belongs to. A dinner for the whole party goes to master; a room-service order to room 204 goes to room 204's folio unless the organiser has said otherwise. When in doubt, post to the room folio — moving a charge to master later is easier than chasing a guest who has left.

Rooms join and leave

Groups change. Two cousins arrive a day late, one couple leaves early, the organiser adds three rooms on the second night. Your system needs to add and remove rooms from the group without breaking the master folio, and show availability while doing it.

Check-out

  1. Each guest settles their room folio — usually small.
  2. The organiser reviews the master folio line by line.
  3. The master folio is invoiced to the organiser, with their GSTIN if they gave one, and settled against the advance.

Room rent on the master folio still follows the per-night GST bands: 5% for rooms charged up to ₹7,500 a night, 18% above.

Common problems

  • No group record — fifteen separate bookings that nobody can see together.
  • Charges on the wrong folio, discovered at check-out.
  • Advance not linked to the group, so it is hard to settle against.
  • Released rooms not released — held for a group that never confirmed them, unsold.

A worked example: a 12-room wedding party

The bride's family books 12 rooms for two nights at ₹4,500 a night. They agree to pay room rent and the welcome dinner; guests pay for everything else.

Advances and cancellations

  • Take a meaningful advance at booking, recorded against the group, not a single room.
  • Set a release date for unconfirmed rooms, and a cancellation policy with dates.
  • Agree what happens if the group arrives with fewer rooms than blocked.
  • Issue receipts for every advance, and adjust them on the final invoice.

Corporate groups are different

Corporate groups usually want one GST invoice with the company's GSTIN, often settled after the stay on credit terms. Make sure the company's GSTIN is validated before check-out, decide who approves credit, and track the amount in your outstanding dues report until it is paid.

Common questions

What is a master folio in a hotel?
A master folio is a bill attached to a group booking rather than to one room. Charges the group organiser has agreed to cover, such as room rent or a group dinner, are posted to it, while individual guests' personal charges go to their own room folios.
How do hotels bill a group booking?
Typically the organiser receives one invoice from the master folio for the agreed charges, and each guest settles their own room folio for extras at check-out. Agree in writing beforehand exactly which charges go where.
How do you block rooms for a group?
Create a group booking for the dates, allocate the rooms or room types needed, and set a cut-off date after which unconfirmed rooms are released back to general sale.
