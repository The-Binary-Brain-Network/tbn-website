C-Form, now Form-III: reporting foreign guests in 2026.

Every hotel that accepts foreign guests has a legal duty to tell the immigration authorities they are there — within 24 hours. The form most hoteliers know as the C-Form was renamed Form-III when the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 came into force. The obligation did not get lighter.

Short answer

When a foreign national (including OCI cardholders, under the new rules) stays at your hotel, guest house or homestay, you must submit their details electronically — Form-III, formerly called the C-Form — within 24 hours of arrival, through the Bureau of Immigration's portal (indianfrro.gov.in) or app. Departure must also be reported. Capture passport and visa details at check-in so the form can be filed the same day.

What changed in 2025

The Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 replaced the older foreigners and passport laws and came into force on 1 September 2025. Under its rules, the duty of an accommodation provider to report foreign guests continues, with the form now called Form-III. Hoteliers, police notices and software still call it the C-Form, and we use both names here.

Three points stand out in the published notices:

  • 24 hours. Form-III must be submitted electronically within 24 hours of the guest's arrival.
  • Wider coverage. The duty applies to hotels, guest houses, homestays, hostels and other accommodation that houses foreign nationals, and notices indicate OCI cardholders are now included.
  • Departure too. Several notices require departure details to be reported within 24 hours as well.

Rules are applied by state and district police and the Bureau of Immigration, so read the notice for your area. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

Where to file

Form-III is filed online through the Bureau of Immigration's portal, indianfrro.gov.in, or its designated app. The property registers once — the user ID is approved by the local Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO) or Foreigners Registration Officer — and then files a form for each foreign guest.

What to capture at check-in

The form is only fast to file if the front desk collects everything when the guest arrives. A check-in checklist for a foreign national:

  • Full name as on passport, nationality, date of birth, gender
  • Passport number, place and date of issue, expiry date
  • Visa number, type, place of issue and validity
  • Date of arrival in India and at your property
  • Arrived from (city and country) and next destination
  • Address and contact in the home country, and contact in India
  • Purpose of visit and expected length of stay
  • A photograph, and scans of the passport photo page and visa

Store passport scans securely and limit who can see them. They are sensitive personal data.

A same-day routine

  1. At check-in, the receptionist captures the details above and scans the documents.
  2. Within the shift, the form is submitted on the portal and the acknowledgement recorded against the booking.
  3. At the night audit, the duty manager checks that every foreign guest who arrived today has a submitted form.
  4. At check-out, departure is reported.

The failure mode is always the same: a late-night arrival whose form is "done tomorrow" and then forgotten. A register that flags any foreign guest without a submitted form, before the 24 hours run out, is the cheapest insurance a hotel can buy.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting for a batch. Filing all the week's forms on Saturday breaches the 24-hour rule for most of them.
  • Missing OCI guests on the assumption they are exempt.
  • Forgetting departures.
  • Typing passport numbers twice — once in the hotel system, once on the portal. Every retype is a chance for a wrong digit.
  • No record of submission. If the police ask, you need to show when each form was filed.

A worked example

A German couple arrive at a 20-room hotel in Jaipur at 11.40pm on a Friday. The night receptionist is alone at the desk.

  1. 11.40pm — at check-in, the receptionist scans both passports and visa pages and captures the details in the hotel system, along with their arrival date in India, previous city and next destination.
  2. 11.55pm — once the guests are in their room, the receptionist submits two Form-III entries on the portal and records the acknowledgement numbers against the booking.
  3. Saturday morning — at the night audit review, the manager checks the register: two foreign arrivals, two forms submitted. Nothing pending.
  4. Monday, check-out — departure is reported the same day.

The risky version of the same night: details written on paper, form "to be done by the day shift", the day shift busy with check-outs, and the 24 hours quietly expiring on Saturday night.

Data protection, too

Passport scans and visa details are personal data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Treat them accordingly:

  • Store scans inside your hotel system, not in a receptionist's WhatsApp or phone gallery.
  • Limit who can view full passport numbers; mask them in lists where possible.
  • Keep records only as long as the law and your accounts require.
  • Never share guest documents with anyone except the authorities entitled to them.

Questions police and FRRO officers commonly ask

  • Is the property registered on the portal, and who holds the login?
  • Can you show the Form-III submission for a particular guest and when it was filed?
  • Were departures reported?
  • Do you have copies of the guest's passport and visa?

A register that answers each of those in seconds turns an inspection into a formality.

Common questions

What is Form-III for hotels?
Form-III, formerly known as the C-Form, is the report an accommodation provider must submit to the immigration authorities when a foreign national stays with them. Under the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 and its rules, it must be submitted electronically within 24 hours of arrival.
What details are needed for a C-Form or Form-III?
Typically the guest's name, nationality, passport number, place and date of issue and expiry, visa number, type and validity, date of arrival in India and at the hotel, address in their home country, purpose of visit, next destination and expected departure, plus a photograph. The portal lists the current fields.
Do homestays and guest houses need to file C-Forms?
Yes. The obligation applies to accommodation providers broadly, including hotels, guest houses, homestays, hostels and others that house foreign nationals.
What is the penalty for not filing a C-Form?
The 2025 Act provides for substantial fines and possible imprisonment for accommodation providers who fail to report. Published local notices cite fines up to ₹1 lakh; check the current rules for your state.
