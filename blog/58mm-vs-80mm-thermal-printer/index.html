58mm vs 80mm thermal printer: which should you buy?

The two common thermal printer widths look almost the same on the shop shelf. On a GST bill with item names, quantities, rates and a tax breakup, the difference between 48mm and 72mm of printable width is the difference between a readable bill and a cramped one.

Short answer

Choose 80mm for restaurant bills, KOTs and any bill with many items or a tax breakup: it prints about 72mm wide, enough for item, quantity, rate and amount on one line. Choose 58mm for compact counters with short bills, where space and cost matter more: it prints about 48mm wide. Check that your billing software supports the width before buying.

What fits on a line

A restaurant bill line usually wants four things: item name, quantity, rate and amount.

On 80mm: Paneer Butter Masala 2 290.00 580.00 — one line.

On 58mm, the same item usually wraps: the name on one line, quantity and amount on the next. That is fine for a café receipt with five items. On a family dinner with eighteen items and a tax breakup, it doubles the bill's length and halves its readability.

When 80mm is the right choice

  • Restaurant bills with many items.
  • KOTs, where large, readable item names matter in a busy kitchen.
  • Hotel bills and room-service bills with a tax breakup, dates and a room number.
  • Anywhere you need an auto-cutter at speed.

When 58mm is enough

  • Small cafés, juice counters and bakeries with short bills.
  • A hotel desk where room-service bills are short and space is tight.
  • Portable or Bluetooth billing.
  • You already own one, and it works.

The last point is the most common one: many small properties already have a 58mm printer from a previous system. If your software supports it, there is no reason to replace it.

Connectivity

  • USB — simplest, must be near the computer.
  • Ethernet — best for kitchens and anywhere away from the PC. Give it a fixed IP address.
  • Wi-Fi — flexible, but kitchens are hard on signals.
  • Bluetooth — common on 58mm portable models, for phones and tablets.

Check the software first

Not every billing system supports both widths. Before buying, confirm:

  • The software lets you choose 58mm or 80mm.
  • Your printer model works with it — ideally, it is set up automatically.
  • Margins can be adjusted if a few characters are clipped.
  • It can send a cut command directly if the driver ignores it.

If prints come out clipped or in a narrow strip, it is almost always a width mismatch between the driver and the software — see thermal printer troubleshooting.

Paper: the running cost nobody budgets

Thermal paper is the only consumable a thermal printer has, and it is easy to underestimate. A busy restaurant printing a bill and two or three KOTs per order can use several rolls a day across all its printers. A few points save money and trouble:

  • Roll length matters more than width. Two 80mm rolls can look identical and hold very different lengths of paper. Compare price per metre, not per roll.
  • Core size and outer diameter must fit your printer. Check the printer's maximum roll diameter before buying in bulk.
  • Bills fade. Standard thermal paper fades over months, faster in heat and sunlight. If you keep printed bills for your records, store them in a cool, dark file — or better, rely on the software's stored copy and reprint when needed.
  • Cheap paper costs more. Poor-quality rolls leave dust that wears the print head. A new head costs more than a year's difference in paper price.

Shorter bills on any width

Whatever width you choose, a few layout decisions make bills shorter and clearer:

  • Keep the header to name, address, GSTIN and FSSAI — not a paragraph of marketing.
  • Use short, consistent item names in the menu ("Pnr Btr Masala" is too short; "Paneer Butter Masala (Full)" is fine).
  • Show the tax breakup once at the bottom, not per line.
  • Drop a decorative footer if it adds two inches to every bill.
  • Print the hotel or restaurant logo only on the customer copy, if at all.

A quick buying checklist

  1. Decide the width from the bill you print most often, not the biggest one.
  2. Choose the connection by where the printer will sit: USB beside the PC, Ethernet in the kitchen.
  3. Insist on an auto-cutter for any counter that prints more than a few dozen bills a day.
  4. Check your billing software supports the width and the model.
  5. Buy a spare roll stock and a cleaning pen with the printer.

Common questions

What is the difference between 58mm and 80mm thermal printers?
Paper width. An 80mm roll has roughly 72mm printable, and a 58mm roll roughly 48mm. 80mm fits more characters per line, so bills with long item names and tax breakups are easier to read.
Is a 58mm printer enough for a GST bill?
It can be, for short bills with brief item names. Long names wrap onto two lines and the tax breakup takes more length. For restaurants with large orders, 80mm is more comfortable.
Do 58mm printers have auto-cutters?
Many cheaper 58mm printers use a tear bar instead of an auto-cutter. Most 80mm counter printers have an auto-cutter. Check before buying if you need clean cuts at speed.
