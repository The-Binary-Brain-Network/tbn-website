Best restaurant billing software in India: how to shortlist in 2026.

Every vendor's comparison page ends with the vendor winning. This one doesn't try to. It gives you the checks that actually separate a good restaurant billing system from a bad fit, so you can shortlist two or three and trial them properly.

Short answer

The best restaurant billing software for an Indian restaurant is the one that bills correctly under your GST registration, keeps working when the internet drops, routes kitchen tickets the way your kitchen works, and costs a predictable amount over five years. Shortlist on those four, then run a free trial on a real service before paying anything.

Start with what kind of restaurant you are

"Best" depends almost entirely on how you sell. A 12-table family restaurant in Nashik, a cloud kitchen doing 80% of its orders through aggregators, a café chain with four outlets and a bar with a long evening service need different things from the same category of software. Before comparing vendors, write down three facts about yourself:

  • Where your orders come from. Dine-in, takeaway, your own delivery, or Swiggy and Zomato. If aggregators are more than half your volume, deep aggregator integration moves to the top of the list.
  • How many outlets and billing counters you run, now and in two years.
  • How reliable your internet is during your busiest hour. Not on average. During the Friday-night rush.

Those three facts eliminate half the market before you look at a single feature list.

The seven checks that actually separate products

1. Does it bill correctly under your GST registration?

A standalone restaurant charges 5% GST without input tax credit. A restaurant inside a hotel where any room is charged above ₹7,500 a night charges 18% with credit. A restaurant registered under the composition scheme must not charge GST at all — it issues a Bill of Supply with a declaration line instead of a tax invoice. Ask every vendor to show you a printed composition-scheme bill. Many cannot, and a "GST-ready" POS that prints CGST and SGST lines on a composition bill is putting you in breach of the rules on every sale. Our guide to the composition-scheme Bill of Supply covers what it must say.

2. What happens when the internet drops?

Unplug the router during the demo and try to bill a table. Some cloud systems queue orders in the browser and catch up later; some stop. Some print KOTs locally; some cannot reach the printer at all without the server. You want billing, KOT printing and the day's reports to work with no connection. Offline versus cloud POS goes through the trade-off in detail — cloud has real advantages for multi-outlet groups, and we say where.

3. Does kitchen routing match your kitchen?

If your tandoor, Chinese and beverage stations are separate, each needs its own ticket. Good software maps menu categories to stations and prints each station's items at that station's printer — one order, several tickets, no waiter tearing a slip in half. If you want screens instead of paper, ask whether a kitchen display system is included or sold per screen. See KOT routing by station and KDS versus printed KOT.

4. How are split bills and payments handled?

Two different things get called "split". Splitting a bill means one table's order becomes several invoices, each with its own number. Splitting a payment means one invoice is paid partly in cash and partly by UPI. You will need both. Check that each split bill gets its own sequential invoice number — GST requires it.

5. Is UPI confirmation automatic?

A static UPI QR works with any app and needs only your UPI ID, but someone has to check the phone to confirm payment. A dynamic QR through a gateway carries the exact amount and confirms itself. At a busy counter that difference is a queue. UPI QR with auto-confirmation explains the options.

6. Can you get your data out?

Ask how you export sales, items, GST and expenses — and what happens to your data if you stop paying. Excel and PDF exports should be a button, not a support ticket.

7. What does it cost over five years, all-in?

The headline price rarely includes the kitchen display, the inventory module, extra terminals or aggregator integration. Ask for a written five-year quote including everything you will use. Our cost calculator does the maths for a monthly or per-outlet subscription against an annual licence.

Questions vendors tend not to volunteer

  • Which features in the demo are in my plan, and which are add-ons?
  • Is pricing per outlet, per terminal, per user, or per order?
  • Does the price rise at renewal, and is that written into the contract?
  • Where is my data stored, and can I get a full export without asking?
  • What happens to billing if your servers are down?
  • Who do I call at 10pm on a Saturday, and do they pick up?

How to run a trial that tells you something

A demo shows you the vendor's restaurant. A trial shows you yours. Install the shortlisted system on your counter PC, load your real menu, and run at least one full lunch and one full dinner on it alongside your current system. Then check:

  1. How long a typical order takes to punch at peak.
  2. Whether every station received the right ticket.
  3. Whether the bill, printed, has your GSTIN, FSSAI number, correct tax and sequential number.
  4. Whether the day's sales report matches the cash and UPI you actually collected.

If two products pass all four, choose on support and five-year cost.

Common questions

Which is the best restaurant billing software in India?
There is no single best one. Large chains with aggregator-heavy delivery usually need a cloud POS with deep Swiggy and Zomato integration. Single outlets and small groups that want predictable cost and offline billing are often better served by an annual-licence POS that runs on their own PC. Shortlist on GST correctness, offline behaviour, kitchen routing and five-year cost.
How much should restaurant billing software cost?
In India, cloud restaurant POS commonly costs ₹800 to ₹2,500 a month per outlet, and self-hosted annual licences typically ₹6,000 to ₹9,000 a year. Compare five-year totals including every add-on you will use, not the first month's price.
Does restaurant billing software need internet?
It should not need it to bill. A POS that stops issuing bills when the connection drops is a risk during every service. Look for software where billing, KOT printing and reports keep working offline and sync or update when the connection returns.
What features are essential in restaurant billing software?
GST-correct bills (including composition if you are registered under it), table and order management, kitchen order tickets routed by station, split bills and split payments, UPI QR, inventory with recipe deduction, reports your accountant can use, and data you can export.
