GST on hotel rooms in 2026: 5%, 18% and what changed.

The GST Council's 2025 rate rationalisation — widely called GST 2.0 — changed the most common hotel room rate in India. Rooms that used to carry 12% now carry 5%, but with a catch that matters to every small hotel's accounts.

Short answer

Since 22 September 2025, hotel accommodation charged at up to ₹7,500 per room per night attracts 5% GST without input tax credit, and accommodation above ₹7,500 per night attracts 18% with input tax credit. The test is the actual price charged per unit per night. A restaurant inside a hotel where any room is charged above ₹7,500 attracts 18% with credit; otherwise hotel restaurants charge 5%.

The two bands

Price charged per room per nightGSTInput tax credit
Up to ₹7,5005% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST)Not available
Above ₹7,50018% (9% CGST + 9% SGST)Available

These rates apply from 22 September 2025, following the 56th GST Council meeting. Before that date, rooms up to ₹7,500 carried 12% with input tax credit.

What "price charged" means

The band is decided by the actual value charged for the unit per night, not by a published or "declared" tariff. A room listed at ₹9,000 but sold at ₹7,000 on a weekday falls in the 5% band for that night. The same room sold at ₹8,500 on a weekend falls in the 18% band. Your software has to apply the band per night, based on the rate actually billed.

Worked example

A guest stays two nights: Friday at ₹6,800 and Saturday at ₹8,200. Friday: ₹6,800 + 5% = ₹7,140. Saturday: ₹8,200 + 18% = ₹9,676. One stay, two rates — and an invoice that shows both correctly.

The catch: no input tax credit at 5%

Under the old 12% rate, hotels could claim credit for GST paid on their inputs — linen, furniture, renovation, services. At 5%, that credit is not available. For many small hotels the lower rate still helps guests, but the hotel loses credit on its costs.

Commentary on the change also points out that a hotel whose rooms all fall in the 5% band may need to reverse unutilised credit held on 22 September 2025, and that a hotel with rooms in both bands needs to apportion credit. This is exactly the kind of question to settle with your accountant; the hotel industry associations have asked the government to restore credit for the 5% segment, so watch for further changes.

Is anything still exempt?

The old exemption for rooms charged up to ₹1,000 a night was withdrawn from 18 July 2022. Budget rooms are now in the 5% band. Some sources still describe them as exempt; that information is out of date.

Restaurants inside hotels

A restaurant inside a hotel follows a related test. If any unit of accommodation in the hotel is charged above ₹7,500 a night, the premises are "specified premises" and the restaurant charges 18% with input tax credit. Otherwise, it charges 5% without credit, like a standalone restaurant. One premium suite sold above ₹7,500 can therefore change the rate on every dosa in the coffee shop — another reason to watch suite pricing carefully. Our GST guide for restaurants covers the F&B side.

What your hotel software has to do

  • Apply the band per night from the rate actually charged.
  • Let you configure the bands and rates, so a future change is a setting, not a software update.
  • Show CGST and SGST (or IGST for a business guest from another state, where applicable) correctly on the invoice.
  • Produce GSTR-1 data split into B2B, B2C large and B2C small, and an export for your accounting software. See GSTR-1 and Tally export.
  • Print your GSTIN and, where you serve food, your FSSAI number.

Updating your setup

If your system still charges 12% on rooms up to ₹7,500, it has been wrong since 22 September 2025. Check three things this week: the tariff band configuration, an invoice for a room under ₹7,500, and an invoice for a room above it. Then ask your accountant whether any invoices issued at the old rate need correcting.

Invoicing scenarios worth checking

Extra bed or extra person charges

If you charge for an extra bed, consider how it affects the value charged for the unit per night, and therefore the band. Agree the treatment with your accountant and configure the software consistently.

Room plus breakfast packages

A room-with-breakfast rate may be treated as a composite supply, typically taxed at the rate of the principal supply — the room. If your packages straddle the ₹7,500 line, take advice on how to price and invoice them.

Discounts

Because the test is the price actually charged, a discount that brings a ₹8,000 room down to ₹7,200 moves that night into the 5% band. Your software should calculate GST on the post-discount rate.

Business guests from other states

For accommodation, the place of supply is generally where the property is located, so most room invoices carry CGST and SGST even for out-of-state business guests. Confirm edge cases with your accountant.

A checklist for your accountant meeting

  • Which of our rooms and rates fall in each band, by season?
  • Do we need to reverse any input tax credit from September 2025?
  • If we have rooms in both bands, how do we apportion credit?
  • Does any room sold above ₹7,500 change the GST on our restaurant?
  • Are our packages and extra-bed charges invoiced correctly?

Common questions

What is the GST rate on hotel rooms below ₹7,500?
5% without input tax credit, effective 22 September 2025, for accommodation charged at up to ₹7,500 per unit per night. It was previously 12% with input tax credit.
What is the GST rate on hotel rooms above ₹7,500?
18% with input tax credit, for accommodation charged above ₹7,500 per unit per night.
Are hotel rooms under ₹1,000 exempt from GST?
No longer. The exemption for rooms up to ₹1,000 was withdrawn from 18 July 2022. Such rooms now fall in the 5% band.
What GST does a restaurant inside a hotel charge?
If any unit of accommodation in the hotel is charged above ₹7,500 per night, the restaurant is treated as being in specified premises and charges 18% with input tax credit. Otherwise it charges 5% without credit, like a standalone restaurant.
