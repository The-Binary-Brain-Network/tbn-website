GSTR-1 and Tally export for hotels: clean data, no retyping.

Every month a small hotel's accountant asks for the same things: the invoices, split the way GSTR-1 wants them, and something they can load into Tally. When the hotel software produces both, month-end takes an hour instead of two days.

Short answer

GSTR-1 reports a hotel's outward supplies, split into B2B invoices (to GST-registered guests, with their GSTIN), B2C large (inter-state invoices to unregistered guests above the threshold) and B2C small (everything else, summarised by state and rate). Hotel software should export this split directly, along with a file your accountant can import into Tally, so invoices are never retyped.

What GSTR-1 needs from a hotel

GSTR-1 lists your outward supplies for the period. For a hotel, those are room charges, food and beverage, laundry and other services. They are reported in buckets:

BucketWhat goes in itReported as
B2BInvoices to GST-registered guests (companies, travel agents)Each invoice, with the guest's GSTIN
B2C large (B2CL)Inter-state invoices to unregistered guests above the prescribed valueEach invoice
B2C small (B2CS)All other supplies to unregistered guestsTotals by place of supply and rate
Credit/debit notesCorrections to issued invoicesEach note
HSN/SAC summarySupplies by service codeSummary

Thresholds and table formats are revised from time to time; your accountant will apply the current ones.

Where hotel data goes wrong

Missing or wrong GSTINs

A corporate guest who wanted credit gives a GSTIN at check-in; the desk types it with one digit wrong. The invoice goes into B2B with an invalid number, and the guest cannot claim credit. Validate GSTINs at entry — the last character is a check digit, so many typos can be caught immediately.

Place of supply

For hotel accommodation, the place of supply is generally where the property is located, so most room invoices are intra-state (CGST + SGST) even when the guest is from another state. Getting this wrong creates IGST invoices that should not exist. Confirm treatment of specific cases with your accountant.

Rates

Since 22 September 2025, rooms charged up to ₹7,500 a night are at 5% and above that at 18%; F&B follows its own rules. If software is still using old rates, GSTR-1 inherits the error. See GST on hotel rooms.

Edited invoices

An invoice changed after issue, without a credit note or cancel-and-reissue, leaves the return and the accounts disagreeing. Corrections should always produce a new document and keep the original.

The Tally side

Most small hotels' accountants work in Tally. Retyping a month of invoices into Tally is slow and introduces errors. A Tally XML export lets the accountant import sales vouchers — invoice number, date, party, ledger, tax — in one step. Before relying on it, do a trial import with your accountant and agree ledger names, because the export has to match how they have set up your books.

A month-end checklist

  • All night audits for the month have run.
  • No invoices left in draft; corrections issued properly.
  • B2B invoices all carry valid GSTINs.
  • GST report totals match the revenue summary.
  • GSTR-1 export and Tally export generated and sent together.
  • Foreign guests' Form-III records complete (not part of GST, but the same month-end habit).

A worked example: one month, three buckets

A 25-room hotel issues 340 invoices in a month:

  • 42 B2B invoices — to companies and travel agents with GSTINs. Each is reported individually with the customer's GSTIN, so each must be correct.
  • A handful of B2C large invoices, if any meet the current criteria — reported individually.
  • The remainder as B2C small — reported as totals by place of supply and tax rate: for example, rooms at 5%, rooms at 18%, food at 5%.

When the software produces these buckets directly, the accountant checks totals and files. When it does not, someone sorts 340 invoices by hand.

Corrections: credit notes and reissues

Mistakes happen: a guest's company name is wrong, or a charge was posted to the wrong room. The rule of thumb is never to edit an issued invoice silently. Either cancel it and issue a new one — before it has been reported — or issue a credit note against it. Both leave a trail your accountant and an auditor can follow. Ask your accountant which they prefer for which situations, and make sure the software supports it.

Questions to settle with your accountant once

  • Which ledger names should room revenue, F&B and other income map to in Tally?
  • Do they want one Tally voucher per invoice, or daily summaries?
  • How should advances be recorded, and how are they adjusted against invoices?
  • Which SAC codes apply to your services?
  • When do they need the month's data — and in which format?

Write the answers down and configure the export once. Every month after that is routine.

Common questions

What is GSTR-1 for a hotel?
GSTR-1 is the return of outward supplies. A hotel reports its invoices for rooms, food and other services, split into B2B invoices with the customer's GSTIN, large inter-state B2C invoices, and other B2C supplies summarised by place of supply and rate.
What is the difference between B2B, B2CL and B2CS in GSTR-1?
B2B covers invoices to GST-registered customers and is reported invoice by invoice with their GSTIN. B2CL covers inter-state invoices to unregistered customers above the prescribed value. B2CS covers all other supplies to unregistered customers, reported as totals by state and tax rate.
Can hotel software export data to Tally?
Many can. A Tally XML export lets your accountant import sales vouchers directly instead of entering each invoice. Check the format with your accountant before relying on it.
