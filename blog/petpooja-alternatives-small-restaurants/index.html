Petpooja alternatives for small restaurants: a fair comparison.

We build a restaurant POS, so we compete with Petpooja. That makes us a biased source, and you should read this knowing it. We have tried to write the comparison we would want to read as restaurant owners: including the cases where Petpooja is the better choice.

Short answer

Petpooja is one of the most widely used restaurant POS systems in India and a strong choice for restaurants that depend on Swiggy and Zomato and want a large integration catalogue and a cloud dashboard. Smaller restaurants look for alternatives mainly for predictable all-in cost, offline billing, data kept on their own machine, or simpler software. Compare on five-year total cost, offline behaviour and the integrations you actually use.

First, what Petpooja does well

Petpooja is one of the best-known restaurant POS platforms in India, used by a very large number of outlets from single cafés to chains. Its strengths are real:

  • Aggregator integration. If most of your orders come through Swiggy and Zomato, having them land directly in your POS — with menu and stock syncing — saves real effort. This is where Petpooja is strongest.
  • A broad integration catalogue — payment, accounting, CRM, delivery and more.
  • Cloud reporting from your phone, and a head-office view for groups.
  • Scale and support network across many Indian cities.

If those are your priorities, Petpooja deserves a place on your shortlist, and you may well choose it.

Why smaller restaurants look elsewhere

The restaurant owners who talk to us about switching usually give one of four reasons. Check whether any apply to you.

1. The all-in price

Most cloud POS platforms, Petpooja included, offer a core plan plus optional modules — inventory, kitchen display, CRM, integrations, additional terminals. The headline price and the price of the plan you actually need can differ a lot. Ask for a written five-year quote with every module you will use. Our cost guide walks through the arithmetic.

2. Offline behaviour

Ask any cloud vendor exactly what continues to work when the internet drops, and test it in the demo by unplugging the router. Some restaurants in patchy-connectivity locations prefer software that bills entirely from the local PC. See offline versus cloud POS.

3. Data on their own machine

Some owners simply prefer their sales history on their own hardware rather than a vendor's servers.

4. Paying for integrations they don't use

A dine-in restaurant with little delivery business may not need an aggregator-first platform.

Questions to ask any POS vendor, Petpooja included

  • What is the total price for my outlet over five years, with every module I need?
  • Is the kitchen display included, or charged per screen?
  • What exactly works when my internet is down?
  • Can I export all my data to Excel without asking support?
  • Does it print a correct composition-scheme Bill of Supply?
  • Is renewal pricing fixed in writing?

A fair trial: how to compare two POS systems

  1. Load the same menu into both, with variants and modifiers.
  2. Punch the same ten orders in each, timing a typical one.
  3. Print bills from both and compare them line by line: GSTIN, FSSAI, tax, numbering.
  4. Route a multi-station order and check every station received its items.
  5. Unplug the internet and try again.
  6. Export a day's sales and GST reports and show them to your accountant.

Choose the one your counter staff are faster on and your accountant prefers. Price breaks a tie.

Switching POS without losing a day

  • Export your menu, customers and last year's sales from the old system first.
  • Switch at the start of a month, so reports and GST periods line up.
  • Start the new bill series cleanly; note the last invoice number from the old system.
  • Keep the old system readable until your accountant has finished the year.
  • Train staff on a quiet day, not the day of the switch.

Common questions

Is Petpooja good for small restaurants?
It can be. Petpooja is widely used and offers deep aggregator integration and a cloud dashboard. Small restaurants should check the all-in price including the modules they need, how billing behaves offline, and whether they need the integrations they would be paying for.
What are the alternatives to Petpooja?
Broadly: other cloud restaurant POS platforms; desktop or annual-licence POS that runs on your own PC, such as Pass; and simple billing apps for very small outlets. The right type depends on your order channels, outlet count and connectivity.
Which is cheaper than Petpooja?
Prices change and depend on modules, so compare written quotes. As a general pattern, annual-licence software that runs on your own PC costs less over five years than a per-outlet monthly subscription for a single outlet, while cloud platforms can be better value for groups that need their integrations.
