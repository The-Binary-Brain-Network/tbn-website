Restaurant staff attendance and payroll: a simple system that works.

In a small restaurant, payroll is usually a register, a calculator and an argument on the first of the month. It doesn't need to be an HR system — it needs to be a record everyone trusts.

Short answer

A simple restaurant payroll system needs three records: daily attendance (present, absent, half day, leave), salary terms per person (monthly or daily rate), and advances given during the month. Monthly pay is then the earned salary for days worked, minus advances and other deductions. Keep the attendance grid on the same system as billing so it is filled in every day.

Why payroll goes wrong in small restaurants

Restaurant staffing is irregular. People swap shifts, take a half day for a family function, borrow ₹2,000 in the middle of the month, and leave at short notice. When attendance lives in a notebook that someone fills in "later", the month-end conversation becomes a negotiation. The fix is not sophisticated software. It is a record filled in daily, that staff can see, with one agreed formula.

Record 1: daily attendance

A monthly grid — staff down the side, dates across the top — with one code per cell:

  • P — present
  • A — absent
  • H — half day
  • L — leave (paid, if your policy allows)

Fill it in at the start of each shift, not at the end of the month. If the grid lives on the billing computer, the manager opening the counter fills it in as part of opening — which is why keeping attendance on the same system as billing works better than a separate app nobody opens.

Record 2: salary terms

For each person, write down how they are paid — a fixed monthly salary or a daily rate — and any fixed allowances. Agree whether weekly offs are paid, how half days count, and how many paid leave days are allowed. Put it on paper and have the person sign it.

Record 3: advances

Advances are the main cause of payday disputes. Record each one the day it is given — date, amount, and a signature or thumbprint if possible — and decide at the time whether it comes off this month's salary or is spread over several.

The monthly calculation

For a monthly-salaried cook on ₹18,000 in a 30-day month:

ItemValue
Days present24
Half days2 (counted as 1 day)
Paid weekly offs4
Paid days29
Earned salary₹18,000 ÷ 30 × 29 = ₹17,400
Advance taken on the 12th₹2,000
Net pay₹15,400

Whatever method you choose, use it for everyone, every month. Consistency matters more than the exact formula.

The salary sheet

At month end, produce one sheet with a row per person: salary, paid days, earned salary, advances, other deductions, net pay. Print it, have each person sign against their row when paid, and keep a copy. Export it for your accountant, who will handle any statutory deductions.

Statutory deductions

As a restaurant grows, Provident Fund (generally from 20 employees), ESI (generally from 10 employees in notified areas, within wage limits), professional tax (state-specific) and minimum wage rules come into play. A simple attendance-and-salary system gives your accountant clean inputs; it does not replace their advice on compliance.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Filling attendance at month end from memory.
  • Advances given in cash with no record.
  • Different formulas for different people.
  • Paying without a signed salary sheet.
  • Keeping payroll in a personal phone's notes.

Making it fair, and seen to be fair

  • Show each person their attendance at the end of every week, not just at month end.
  • Correct mistakes when they are raised, with a note of why.
  • Pay on the same date every month.
  • Give a printed payslip — even a simple one — with paid days, advances and net pay.
  • Keep salary information private: one person's pay is not the whole team's business.

Most payroll disputes are not about money; they are about not knowing how the number was worked out.

When someone leaves mid-month

Calculate pay for days worked up to the last day, subtract any outstanding advances, and settle on the last day if you can. Record the final settlement and get a signature. If the person leaves without notice, follow the terms you agreed in writing — another reason to have them.

Common questions

How do you calculate salary from attendance?
For a monthly-salaried employee, a common method is: monthly salary ÷ days in the month × paid days, where paid days are days present plus paid leave plus half days counted as 0.5. Then subtract advances and other deductions. Agree the method in writing with staff.
How should salary advances be recorded?
Record each advance on the day it is given, with the amount and the person's acknowledgement. Deduct the total from that month's salary, or spread it across months if agreed. Never deduct an advance that was not recorded.
Do small restaurants need PF and ESI?
It depends on headcount and wages. EPF generally applies to establishments with 20 or more employees, and ESI to those with 10 or more in notified areas, subject to wage limits. Statutory deductions need an accountant's input; a basic salary sheet does not replace payroll compliance.
