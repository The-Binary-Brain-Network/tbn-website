What We Build

The problems we're actually good at.

We work almost entirely in hospitality, food and beverage, and service businesses. That narrowness is the point — it's where our judgement is worth paying for, and where we can tell you in the first conversation whether your idea will survive contact with a real operator.

01

Billing & point-of-sale

GST-compliant invoicing, thermal-printer workflows, sequential audit-safe numbering, split and part payments, UPI QR, corrections that version rather than overwrite. We've built this three times; we know where it goes wrong.

02

Offline-first desktop software

Systems that keep working when the internet doesn't. Local database, encrypted at rest where it matters, automatic backups with a real restore path, and silent updates that never interrupt a shift.

03

Multi-terminal & local network

A counter, a kitchen screen and three phones sharing one live view over the venue's own WiFi — with zero-config discovery, so nobody is typing IP addresses. Devices that drop off queue their work and catch up.

04

Inventory, staff & payroll

Recipe-linked stock that deducts itself, wastage tracking, attendance grids, salary sheets computed from attendance and advances, role-based access with PIN authentication.

05

Indian compliance

GST with flat and per-item rates, GSTIN and FSSAI on documents, GSTR-1 and Tally exports, C-Form and FRRO registers for foreign guests. The unglamorous work that decides whether software is usable in India at all.

06

Internal operational tools

The spreadsheet that's outgrown itself. Handover logs, day-close reconciliation, owner reporting, approval workflows — the systems a growing business runs on but nobody sells off the shelf.

How It Works

Discovery first. Fixed price after.

We don't quote a number before we understand the problem, and we don't bill by the hour — hourly billing makes slow work profitable, which is the wrong incentive to put between us.

01

Conversation — free

Tell us the problem. If an existing product solves it — ours or somebody else's — we'll tell you that and you'll have saved yourself a project. This happens more often than you'd expect from someone selling custom development.

02

Discovery — paid, and yours to keep

We spend time with the people who'll actually use the thing, and produce a written specification: what it does, what it doesn't, how it behaves when things go wrong. You own that document outright. If you take it to another developer, that's a legitimate outcome and there's no clause stopping you.

03

Fixed price against fixed scope

One number, quoted against the specification, staged across milestones. Scope changes are re-quoted openly rather than absorbed into a vague hourly total. You always know what you're committed to.

04

Build, with you seeing it early

Working software in front of your staff as early as it's useful, not a reveal at the end. The people who'll use it daily find the problems that a specification never catches.

05

Handover — code, data and documentation

Source code, database and documentation are yours. We don't hold software hostage as a retention strategy. Ongoing support is a separate agreement you're free to decline, and declining it doesn't cost you access to anything.

Answers

What people ask before starting.

How much does custom software cost in India?
It depends entirely on scope, so any number quoted before scoping is guesswork — and usually a number designed to win the conversation rather than survive it. We run a paid discovery phase that produces a written specification you own, then quote a fixed price against that fixed scope. Not hourly, because hourly billing makes slow work profitable. If discovery concludes that an off-the-shelf product solves your problem, we'll say so and you take the specification elsewhere.
Who owns the source code?
You do. Code, database and documentation are handed over at the end. We don't hold software hostage as a retention mechanism — if you want to take it to another developer in two years, nothing in the arrangement stops you. It's worth asking this question of anyone you're considering, because the answer isn't always yes.
Why should I trust a small team over an established agency?
Partly you shouldn't, without evidence — so here's ours: Folio is running in production at a Mumbai hotel, Pass and Slate are in trials with real businesses. That's shipped software, not a portfolio of work done for clients who may or may not still use it. The trade-off is honest: a larger agency gives you more people and more process. We give you direct access to whoever is writing your code, and a team that has made the mistakes in this domain already, at our own expense.
Do you build web apps, or only desktop software?
Both, but we'll push back on web for anything that must work during a power cut or a dead connection. Our own products are offline-first desktop applications for exactly that reason. Web is the right answer for dashboards, reporting, customer-facing booking and anything genuinely multi-location — and Slate is a hybrid, running a local server that phones and tablets reach over the property's own network.
How long does a project take?
A focused internal tool is usually weeks. A full billing or property-management system is months — Folio took considerably longer than that, and it does one thing. Discovery gives you a real timeline instead of an optimistic one, and we'd rather tell you six months honestly than three months you'll resent us for.
Do you work outside Mumbai?
Yes. The Binary Brain Network is the software division of The Big Brand Network, registered in Mumbai and operating across Delhi NCR and pan-India. Installation and training happen on site where that makes sense; day-to-day work and support run remotely over a direct WhatsApp line.
Can you take over software someone else built?
Sometimes. It depends on what state it's in and whether you have the source code — which is precisely why we hand ours over. We'll look at it honestly and tell you whether maintaining it or rebuilding it is the better use of your money. Occasionally the answer is uncomfortable.
